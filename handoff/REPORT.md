# Art lane: master palette candidates (maker/openjar art-palettes-1)

Branch `feature/art-master-palettes`. Not pushed.

## Headline

Three palettes are done and drawn. Every check passes **except one that no palette can fix**:
the fixed player green `#3CC24A` and player red `#E4393C` are only **ΔE 9.5** apart under a
deuteranopia simulation (target ≥ 20). Both colours are fixed, so `make_palettes.py` exits 1 on
purpose. Someone has to decide whether to change a fixed colour (options below).

## Pick: C, Cool Arcade

Its closest call between a player colour and any sky tier or soil ring is ΔE 29.6, against 27.0
for A and 25.3 for B. That means P1 green and P2 red pop hardest on the TV in every game. If
Kaden prefers the look of B, it still passes, but its teal high-sky is the closest thing to player
green in any palette.

## Things Kaden (or Gary) should know

1. **Deuteranopia: the fixed pair fails, whatever palette he picks.** Machado 2009 (linear RGB)
   gives 9.5. A Viénot/Brettel-style deutan matrix gives 10.1. (Machado applied to gamma-encoded
   values, which isn't the correct way to use it, gives 18.2, still short.) The smallest single
   change that would pass:
   - red `#E4393C` → `#BA272A` (a darker brick red, shift ΔE 10.1), **or**
   - green `#3CC24A` → `#66EC6E` (a lighter green, shift ΔE 10.4).
   I did **not** apply either. Fixed colours are Kaden's call, and the character look sheets in
   `openjar-characters` would need the same change. Until then, a red-green colour-blind player can
   still tell P1 from P2 by lightness (green is much lighter) and silhouette (round hood vs flat hat).
2. **Hat and shirt can't meet the ink rule.** `#2B2B33` and `#1E1E26` are darker than any legal
   outline can be, so 3:1 against ink is impossible. The rule I used, and tested: dark costume parts
   get an inner rim line in the palette's *stone* colour (visible on Chuck in every card). Please
   confirm this as the house rule.
3. **The ink has to be almost black in every palette.** Jeans blue is dark enough that any ink lighter
   than relative luminance 0.0036 fails 3:1. So the three inks differ only in tint. The same rule pushed
   every "outer space" tier to a bright violet/indigo rather than near-black, which also keeps space
   friendly instead of scary.
4. "Cloud deck" is the sky colour at cloud height. The clouds themselves use the light neutral, so the
   ramp can darken at every step.
5. Jeff's comb uses each palette's comb colour, not player red, as his look sheet asks. Skin on the mock
   cards borrows the palette's *clay* colour as a placeholder.
6. The card figures are rough, original stand-ins (a round chicken-hood figure and a flat-hat figure, a
   plain little dino emblem), not library renders. They're only there to judge colour.
